I recommend a **newsletter**. You want credibility with software engineers, enjoy writing, and have two hours a week. A newsletter lets you develop technical ideas in a medium you like. A podcast would make recording and editing—tasks you dislike—central to the project.

Your goal is exploration, so judge the project by useful technical exchanges and sustainable effort. Subscriber growth can remain secondary.

For a starting experiment, I suggest a **six-week pilot: three short issues, published every two weeks**. This cadence is my suggested default; you have not committed to it.

1. Choose three engineering questions from your own experience. Keep each narrow enough to explain with one concrete example.
2. In the first week of each cycle, spend your two hours drafting: the question, your current view, an example, and the trade-off or uncertainty.
3. In the second week, spend one hour checking technical claims and revising, then use the remaining hour to publish and respond to readers. Invite replies with one focused question.
4. After three issues, check whether you stayed within the time budget, enjoyed the work, and received substantive responses from engineers. Continue if the process works; adjust the topics or distribution if engagement is weak.

The main trade-off is less live conversation than a podcast. Reader replies can provide some of that exchange without adding audio production. If setting up email delivery becomes a distraction, publish the same pieces as posts first and add a mailing list later.
