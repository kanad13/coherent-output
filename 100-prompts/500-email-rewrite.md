# Email Rewrite

## Purpose

Transform a messy, repetitive, incomplete, or poorly articulated email or message into a clear, professional, natural, and actionable draft. Preserve every intended point and material uncertainty.

## Inputs

- Source email, message, or notes
- Recipient or relationship, when known
- Desired outcome, when known
- Tone or sensitivity constraints, when known

Do not repeatedly ask for missing context. If a missing fact would materially change the message, ask one focused question. Otherwise state the assumption and proceed. Never invent names, dates, promises, facts, or commitments.

## Workflow

### 1. Build the Communication Brief

Read the complete source and identify:

- Sender's purpose
- Recipient and relationship
- Desired reader action
- Every distinct fact, request, decision, concern, and commitment
- Deadlines, dates, names, and dependencies
- Tone signals and sensitive points
- Repetition, contradictions, ambiguity, and missing information

**Visible output — Communication Brief:**

- **Purpose:**
- **Required points:**
- **Requested action:**
- **Tone:**
- **Assumptions or ambiguities:**

This is the inspectable product of the analysis. Do not provide hidden private chain-of-thought.

### 2. Create the Message Plan

- Deduplicate repeated points without losing nuance.
- Group closely related ideas.
- Sequence them in the order most useful to the recipient:
  1. Purpose or context
  2. Essential facts
  3. Decision, request, or proposed action
  4. Deadline or next step
- Decide which details belong in bullets and which belong in prose.
- State any meaningful tone or sequencing decision and why it helps.

**Visible output — Message Plan:** A concise ordered outline.

### 3. Draft the Email

- Provide a useful subject line for email input.
- Lead with the message's purpose.
- Use short, active sentences.
- Keep one main idea per sentence.
- Use specific language.
- Remove filler, repetition, business jargon, and unnecessary apology.
- Preserve hedging when it communicates real uncertainty, diplomacy, or a non-final commitment.
- Use a professional, direct, and human tone.
- Make the requested action and deadline unmistakable.
- End naturally without formulaic padding.

Do not make the sender sound more certain, aggressive, apologetic, or committed than the source supports.

### 4. Audit the Draft

Verify:

- Every required point appears exactly once in the best location.
- Names, dates, figures, commitments, and modality match the source.
- The recipient can identify the purpose and requested action quickly.
- No unsupported fact or promise was added.
- The tone fits the relationship and subject.
- The message is concise without losing necessary context.

## Output

Return in this order:

1. Communication Brief
2. Message Plan
3. Final email or message in a copy-ready block
4. Brief preservation and tone audit
